# 1. Executive Summary
The RIG trade was built around a long-duration reversal thesis rather than a short-term catalyst. The core observation was that a multi-year downtrend had transitioned into a broad accumulation/base structure. A rounded bottom, improving momentum, a weekly descending-channel break, expanding volume, and Fibonacci reclaim levels were used together to define the trade. Long-dated calls created convex exposure to the early phase of the structural repricing.
The trade succeeded because the entry was taken before the higher-timeframe move was fully repriced, while the exit process became progressively more defensive as the calls moved in-the-money and their behavior shifted from highly convex breakout exposure toward higher-delta exposure. Profit was harvested in stages rather than requiring the entire thesis to reach its most ambitious target.
| Central lesson The repeatable edge was not simply "RIG went up." It was the combination of higher-timeframe base recognition + objective confirmation + asymmetric option selection + aggressive profit protection after convexity had already paid. |
# 2. How the Trade Was Charted
## 2.1 Monthly / secular structure
Multi-year decline from the 2014-2015 area created a long supply-clearing process rather than a simple V-shaped reversal
A broad rounded base was drawn through the 2020-2025 lows, emphasizing the change from persistent lower lows toward stabilization and a rising right-hand side
The base was treated as a regime-transition structure: distribution/downtrend -> accumulation -> potential markup
Long-duration Fibonacci levels were overlaid to create objective price zones rather than relying on an unconstrained upside narrative
## 2.2 Weekly structure
A descending channel/trendline captured the 2023-2025 corrective structure
Price eventually broke above that declining resistance, providing a more actionable confirmation that the right side of the base was developing
Weekly higher lows and reclaim behavior were used as evidence that sellers were losing control
Later charts showed price reclaiming the ~0.382 retracement region around the upper-$5s / low-$6s, with the next major zones near the 0.50 retracement (~$7.3) and higher retracement/supply areas

# 3. Confirmation Stack
## Volume
Volume expanded materially on the right side of the base and around breakout attempts. The thesis treated this as confirmation that the move was attracting participation rather than drifting higher on declining interest.
## MACD / momentum
Monthly and weekly MACD behavior improved from compressed/depressed conditions. The important feature was the direction of change: momentum was turning upward while price was breaking out of a long corrective structure.
## RSI
RSI strengthened into the 50s/60s on the higher timeframes. This was interpreted as constructive trend momentum rather than immediate exhaustion. Later, as price extended, RSI became more useful as a tool for identifying potential exhaustion/divergence near supply.
## Confluence theory
No single indicator created the trade. The setup became compelling because price structure, trendline break, Fibonacci location, volume, MACD and RSI all pointed toward the same regime shift. The indicators were confirmation tools; price structure remained primary.

# 5. Trade Management and Decision Process
## 5.1 Recognition that option returns were decelerating
At roughly +25% on the underlying and approximately +218% on the calls, the option percentage gains appeared to slow. This was correctly recognized as a change in the option payoff profile rather than necessarily a bearish signal. As the $5.50 calls became ITM, they increasingly behaved like leveraged stock: more of the value was intrinsic/delta-driven, while the dramatic convex repricing phase was behind the position.
## 5.2 Volatility-crush concern
IV compression became a legitimate management concern. Long calls retain positive vega, so falling implied volatility can reduce extrinsic value even while the underlying holds. Because the calls were ITM, intrinsic value provided a cushion, but sideways price action plus falling IV would have been an inefficient environment for continuing to hold the full five-contract position.
## 5.3 Vertical-spread concept considered
A bull call vertical was explored as a way to monetize some upside and reduce vega/theta exposure while preserving participation. The initial conceptual structure was long $5.50 / short $9.00, but the June chain did not list a $9 strike. A $10 short call offered too little premium, while the $7 call offered materially better premium and therefore a more meaningful hedge/cap.
The contemplated structure became: sell two calls outright, convert two existing $5.50 calls into $5.50/$7 bull call spreads, and leave one uncapped runner. This was not ultimately executed because the existing long options were held in an account that could not support the desired spread implementation. This distinction matters: the vertical was a risk-management idea, not part of the realized trade.

# 6. Theory Behind the Management
Harvest convexity after it pays. The original reason for owning five calls was the asymmetric early breakout. Once the move delivered >200% option gains, preserving all five contracts was no longer necessary to maintain upside exposure
Separate thesis from instrument. A bullish RIG thesis does not require continuously holding the same option structure. The instrument can be resized or changed as delta, vega, theta and price location change
Use partial exits to reduce binary decision-making. Selling four of five converted the final contract from a portfolio-risk problem into a low-pressure runner
Manage the runner by underlying structure, not by the option P/L percentage. An option can retrace substantially from IV compression while the underlying breakout remains valid
Profit protection should increase as asymmetry decreases. The trade began with low premium and large upside optionality; after a 3x+ move in the option, the remaining reward/risk was fundamentally different

## What worked
Higher-timeframe thinking prevented overreacting to daily noise
Entry was early enough that the options still had meaningful convexity
The rounded-base thesis was confirmed rather than traded in isolation
Volume and momentum supported the breakout rather than contradicting it
Fibonacci levels provided predetermined areas for reassessment and target mapping
Partial exits monetized the trade while preserving optionality
The trader recognized changing option behavior (ITM transition and IV compression) rather than assuming a 218% winner should continue compounding at the same rate
Execution remained flexible when the proposed spread was operationally unavailable; the position was simplified instead of forcing an inappropriate order
## What could be improved
Define the complete exit architecture before entry: e.g., first trim, second trim, runner rule, and whether vertical conversion is permitted in the actual account
Confirm account-level spread permissions and which account holds the options before planning an adjustment
Record exact entry date, underlying entry price, IV, delta, theta and vega at entry so future trades can be compared quantitatively
Record every exit fill, including the final runner, so total realized return and maximum favorable/adverse excursion can be calculated precisely
Distinguish clearly between structural targets and projected drawing paths; the curve drawn on a chart is a scenario visualization, not a forecast that price must follow
# 8. Repeatable Playbook: How to Emulate This Setup
## Phase A - Find the structure
Scan monthly/weekly charts for securities emerging from multi-year declines into long bases
Look for a rounded or multi-stage accumulation structure with a visibly strengthening right side
Draw the dominant declining trendline/channel and identify what price must reclaim to change regime
Anchor Fibonacci to a defensible major swing and mark retracement zones before entering
## Phase B - Demand confirmation
Price breaks/accepts above the declining structure
Volume expands on the breakout or on the right side of the base
Weekly momentum improves; MACD turns/crosses constructively and RSI moves into a trend-supportive regime
Price begins holding reclaimed levels as support rather than immediately falling back into the base
## Phase C - Select the instrument
Use shares when the expected move is small/tactical and option friction would consume too much of the edge
Use long-dated calls when the setup is early, asymmetry is large, and enough time is needed for the structural thesis to develop
Consider verticals later in the move when IV is elevated, upside targets are better defined, and reducing vega/theta exposure is worth capping part of the upside
Never choose the option solely because the percentage payoff looks larger; liquidity, spread width, expiration, IV and strike placement matter
## Phase D - Pre-plan management
Define structural invalidation on the underlying before entry
Define at least two profit-taking zones from chart structure/Fibonacci rather than arbitrary P/L percentages
Decide in advance what portion is core, what portion is tactical, and whether a runner will remain
After a large convex move, explicitly reassess whether the option has transitioned into mostly delta/intrinsic exposure
If most of the thesis has played out, pay yourself; do not require the final target for the trade to count as successful

# 11. Bottom Line
The RIG trade is a useful model because the success came from process at multiple stages: identifying a genuine higher-timeframe regime change, using options when convexity was attractive, validating the move with price/volume/momentum, and then becoming more defensive once the option payoff profile changed. The most reusable principle is to enter when asymmetry is greatest and progressively reduce dependence on the perfect outcome as the market pays the thesis.
For future trades, the goal is not to copy RIG or its exact indicators. The goal is to reproduce the sequence: structure -> confirmation -> asymmetric instrument -> predetermined levels -> staged monetization -> structure-based runner management -> complete post-trade documentation.
