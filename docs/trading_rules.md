# Trading Rules and Macro Analysis Framework

_Source: `trading_rules_macro_analysis_framework.docx`, ingested 2026-09-24._
_Companion to [trading_framework.md](trading_framework.md) (the system backbone). This doc is the
execution rule set: entries, exits, trim percentages, sizing by grade, and the A-F grading scale._

# Purpose
This document outlines a rule-based trading framework for evaluating entries, exits, position sizing, risk tolerance, and macro alignment. The goal is to create a consistent system for reviewing asset setups, grading trade quality, and making disciplined decisions before entering, managing, or exiting a position.
The framework is conservative by default. High grades should be earned through multiple confirmations across technical structure, risk/reward, volume, momentum, and macro conditions. The objective is not to force trades, but to identify high-quality opportunities where the thesis, entry, invalidation, and upside are clearly defined.
# Core Trading Philosophy
Prioritize quality over frequency
Do not chase extended moves without a defined invalidation level
Treat every trade as invalid until the setup proves itself
Require clear technical structure before entering
Use macro analysis to support or weaken the trade thesis
Define exits before entering the trade
Scale out into strength rather than trying to perfectly time tops
Protect capital first, then pursue upside
Grade setups conservatively and avoid overrating trades without confirmation
# Trade Eligibility Rules
A trade should only be considered when most of the following conditions are present:
The asset has a clear directional bias
Price is near a logical entry zone
The invalidation level is obvious before entering
The risk/reward is favorable
Volume, momentum, or price action supports the setup
The trade has room to move before the next major resistance or support zone
The macro environment does not directly conflict with the trade thesis
The setup is not overly extended from support or moving averages
The position size can be managed without excessive emotional or portfolio risk
If these conditions are not present, the asset should remain on watchlist rather than being treated as an actionable trade.
# Entry Rules
## Rule 1: Enter only when the invalidation level is clear
Every trade must have a defined level where the thesis is considered wrong. If the stop or invalidation level cannot be identified before entry, the trade should be downgraded or avoided.
## Rule 2: Prefer confirmation over impulse
Entries should be based on confirmation, not emotional reaction. Confirmation may include:
Breakout above resistance with volume
Pullback into support that holds
Reclaim of a key level after a failed breakdown
Consolidation above support followed by continuation
Bullish or bearish reversal confirmation at a major level
Volume expansion supporting the direction of the trade
## Rule 3: Avoid late entries
A strong setup can become a weak trade if the entry is too extended. If price has already moved far from the ideal entry zone, the setup should be downgraded even if the thesis remains valid.
## Rule 4: Match entry type to setup type
Breakout entry: price clears a key resistance level with volume
Pullback entry: price retests support, moving averages, trendlines, or prior breakout levels
Reclaim entry: price loses and then regains a key level
Continuation entry: price consolidates above support and resumes trend
Reversal entry: price shows exhaustion, divergence, capitulation, or strong reaction from a major support or resistance zone
Reversal trades should require stronger confirmation than continuation trades because they usually carry higher risk.
# Exit Rules
## Rule 1: Define exits before entering
First target
Second target
Invalidation level
Conditions for trimming
Conditions for full exit
Conditions for holding a runner
## Rule 2: Scale out into strength
Trim 20% to 30% at the first major target
Trim another 20% to 30% near the second resistance or extension zone
Hold the remaining 40% to 60% only if the trend remains intact
## Rule 3: Do not let strong gains turn into losses
Move the stop to breakeven
Raise the stop below a higher support level
Trim part of the position
Reduce exposure if momentum weakens
## Rule 4: Exit when the thesis breaks
Price loses the original thesis level
The asset breaks key support or resistance in the wrong direction
Momentum fails after a breakout
Volume confirms a reversal against the position
Macro conditions materially change
The original reason for entering is no longer valid
# Percentage-Based Position Management
## Standard Trim Framework
First target: sell 20% to 30% of the position
Second target: sell another 20% to 30% of the position
Runner position: hold 40% to 60% if the trend remains intact
## Speculative or High-Volatility Assets
Take partial profits earlier
Use smaller starting size
Avoid adding after sharp upside moves
Raise stops more quickly after favorable movement
Do not allow volatility to override the original invalidation level
## High-Conviction Macro Positions
Wider stops may be acceptable
Position size should still reflect downside risk
Trims should still be used to protect capital
The position can be held longer if macro and technical structure remain aligned
The trade should still be exited if the original thesis is invalidated
# Risk Tolerance Rules
| Conviction Level | Position Size | Stop / Invalidation | Management Style |
| Low conviction | Small | Tight invalidation | Avoid averaging down, take profits quickly, exit quickly if setup does not confirm |
| Medium conviction | Moderate | Below key support or above key resistance | Scale out at predefined targets and hold only while structure remains intact |
| High conviction | Larger but controlled | Wider if technical and macro alignment are strong | Use staged trims, avoid oversized exposure, and allow movement only while thesis remains intact |
| Long-term macro | Thesis-based sizing | Wider tolerance but still technically defined | Macro thesis must remain intact and major breakdowns should not be ignored |
# Position Sizing Rules
## Setup-Based Sizing
| Setup Grade | Sizing Rule |
| A setup | Full planned position size |
| B setup | Half to three-quarter size |
| C setup | Small starter position or watchlist only |
| D setup | Avoid unless conditions improve |
| F setup | No trade |
## Sizing Adjustment Rules
Reduce position size when entry is extended, the stop is too far away, macro conditions are mixed, volume confirmation is weak, the asset is highly volatile, the market is risk-off, or there is major catalyst risk ahead.
Increase position size only when entry is near support or has clean breakout confirmation, invalidation is clearly defined, risk/reward is attractive, volume and momentum support the move, macro conditions align, and the setup has multiple confirmations.
# Macro Analysis Rules
Macro analysis should be used to strengthen, weaken, or filter trade ideas. Macro context does not replace technical execution.
## Macro Inputs to Review
Sector trend
Broader market trend
Interest rate environment
Dollar strength or weakness
Inflation expectations
Commodity cycle
Liquidity conditions
Risk-on versus risk-off sentiment
Relative strength versus the broader market
Upcoming catalysts
Correlation to major macro themes
## Macro Alignment Grades
| Grade | Definition |
| Strong | Macro conditions directly support the trade thesis |
| Mixed | Macro conditions are neutral or partially conflicting |
| Weak | Macro conditions work against the trade thesis |
A strong technical setup should be downgraded if macro conditions are clearly working against it. A strong macro thesis still requires a clean technical entry.
# Technical Analysis Rules
## Trend Structure
Higher highs and higher lows for bullish structure
Lower highs and lower lows for bearish structure
Sideways consolidation for neutral structure
Failed breakdown or failed breakout signals
Compression before expansion
## Key Levels
Major support
Major resistance
Breakout level
Retest level
Invalidation level
First target
Second target
## Momentum and Volume
Whether momentum supports the trade direction
Whether volume is expanding on breakouts
Whether volume is drying up during pullbacks
Whether exhaustion signals are present
Whether divergence exists between price and momentum
# Trade Grading System
Each setup should be graded conservatively across trend structure, entry quality, risk/reward, volume confirmation, momentum confirmation, macro alignment, catalyst support, downside risk, distance from invalidation, and overall confidence.
| Grade | Definition |
| A | Strong structure, clean entry, defined invalidation, favorable risk/reward, strong confirmation, supportive macro backdrop, and multiple confirmations |
| B | Good setup with mostly favorable structure, acceptable risk/reward, some confirmation, and one or two missing or mixed factors |
| C | Watchlist setup with unclear or extended entry, incomplete confirmation, or mixed macro conditions |
| D | Weak setup with poor structure, unclear invalidation, unattractive risk/reward, or conflicting macro conditions |
| F | No trade due to broken structure, no clear invalidation, poor risk/reward, or unsupported thesis |
# Pre-Trade Checklist
What is the trade thesis?
What is the macro backdrop?
What level confirms the setup?
What level invalidates the setup?
What is the ideal entry zone?
What is the first profit target?
What is the second profit target?
What is the expected risk/reward?
Is the entry early, fair, or late?
What would cause the trade to be closed?
What percentage of the position will be trimmed at each target?
What grade does the setup deserve?
If these questions cannot be answered clearly, the trade should remain on watchlist.

# Asset Review Template
Asset: ticker or asset name
Bias: bullish, neutral, or bearish
Macro Thesis: brief summary of whether macro supports the trade
Technical Structure: trend, support, resistance, and current price behavior
Entry Plan: preferred entry zone and confirmation required
Invalidation: price level or condition that breaks the thesis
Profit Targets: Target 1, Target 2, and runner target if applicable
Position Management: planned trim percentages and stop adjustment logic
Grade: conservative setup grade with short rationale
Final Decision: trade, watchlist, avoid, trim, hold, or exit
# Final Decision Rules
| Decision | Use When |
| Trade | The setup has strong technical structure, clear invalidation, favorable risk/reward, and supportive macro conditions |
| Watchlist | The thesis is interesting but entry, confirmation, or risk/reward is not yet strong enough |
| Avoid | The setup is extended, unclear, low-quality, or macro conditions conflict with the thesis |
| Trim | The asset reaches a planned target, becomes extended, or risk needs to be reduced |
| Hold | The trade remains technically valid and macro conditions still support the thesis |
| Exit | The thesis breaks, invalidation is hit, or macro conditions materially change against the position |
# Summary
This framework combines technical analysis, macro context, risk tolerance, percentage-based exits, and conservative trade grading. Every trade should have a defined thesis, clear entry, clear invalidation, planned exit levels, and appropriate position sizing. The macro view should support the decision, but technical execution remains required. The system is designed to protect capital, reduce emotional decision-making, and create consistency across asset reviews.
